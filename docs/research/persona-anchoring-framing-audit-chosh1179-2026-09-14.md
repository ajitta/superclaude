# Persona prompting, anchoring and framing: evidence review and `src/superclaude` audit

What this settles: what the 2025–2026 literature actually shows about expert-persona prompts, prompt-embedded anchors and instruction framing; where `src/superclaude` already matches that evidence; and the short list of edits that the evidence supports. The trigger was Andrei Nita's May 2026 post "You Are a Senior Engineer: Why That Prompt Is Wrong" (https://andreinita.co/blog/persona-prompting-expert-panel/). Nothing in `src/` was changed by this study; every proposal below carries its own verification path.

## 1. Sources and how far each was verified

The WebFetch summarizer fabricated numbers at least once during this study (it reported a "79% verdict flip" and a "skeptical persona mitigation" for the EMNLP paper; neither exists in the paper's text), and the first draft of this table still carried four rows (S3, S7, S9, S11) whose numbers had come through that summarizer. After review, on 2026-09-14, every number and quotation in the arXiv rows and in S1 was re-located in raw fetched text: curl, tag-strip, exact-string search, PDF through pypdf where arXiv has no HTML rendering, one pass per source plus an independent refutation pass for S1, S2 and S3. Rows were reworded wherever the raw text disagreed with the summarizer, and each row now says what the paper prints and what is inference. Level A: full text fetched and each cited number or phrase located by exact-string search. Level B: abstract-level claims, located the same way. Level C: secondary summary (search snippet, news article), unverified against the paper.

## 2. What transfers to a content framework

F1. **An expert persona is a lens, not a fact source.** On hard factual questions personas leave accuracy unchanged or lower (S2, S3); longer and more detailed personas hurt more (S3); persona details irrelevant to the task cost up to ~30 points (S4). Where personas help is generative and stylistic work, and tasks where a structured expert frame is itself the deliverable (S3, S5). Anthropic's own wording matches: a role "focuses behavior and tone" (S15). None of the measurements used a current Claude model; the direction is consistent across GPT, Gemini, Qwen and Mistral, so treat it as the prior, not as a Claude measurement.

F2. **Panels earn their cost only through disagreement and distinct criteria.** Multi-persona debate does not beat self-consistency by default and is hyperparameter-sensitive; the lever that moved results was the agreement level (S7). Grounded, information-asymmetric agents beat abstract role-play (S17). S1's usable content is exactly this: the "note explicitly where you disagree" clause, "be very unconvinced", and "each pass must find different things; escalate the frame, not the seniority". These are practitioner claims; S7 and S17 give them a mechanism.

F3. **Skepticism has to be explicit, and side-by-side beats sequential.** Models rarely critique a premise unprompted (S14). An evaluator flips more when the challenge arrives as a conversational follow-up than when alternatives are presented together, and detailed reasoning persuades even when wrong (S13). A reviewer that receives the author's rationale as a follow-up is therefore in the weakest configuration the literature tested.

F4. **Anchors bind, and "ignore the anchor" does not unbind them.** Numbers, hints and expert opinions embedded in a prompt shift the answer (S8, S9); the instruction to ignore them, chain-of-thought and reflection barely help (S8); reasoning lowers the effect but does not remove it (S9). Two structural remedies have measured support: establish the model's own criterion before it sees the anchor (S9's best mitigation), and collect several independent angles instead of one hint (S8). A number labeled as a prior AI estimate anchors harder than the same bare number (S10, level C).

F5. **Framing moves choices, including under thinking.** Logically equivalent framings produced a 4% vs 69% split across 82 models, and the Claude family moved 0.71 (S11). Binary questions lean "no" (S12). For instructions, the vendor evidence favors positive phrasing for formatting and concision (S15, S16); the general "never use negatives" claim rests on anecdote. Position effects are real in mechanism (S18) but the popular compliance percentages are unsourced.

F6. **The description field is a persona.** The repo's own experiment (S17) showed the description alone changes hallucination rate and code shape; that is the same "irrelevant persona detail" effect S4 measured, seen from the authoring side.

## 3. Audit of `src/superclaude`

Method: every agent `<role>` and `description`, every mode, the panel commands, `/sc:prompt`, `/sc:review`, `/sc:estimate`, `/sc:brainstorm`, `core/rules/*`, `core/BUSINESS_SYMBOLS.md`, the authoring rules under `.claude/rules/`, and `context_loader.py`'s injection order were read against F1–F6. Tags are named instead of line numbers because lines move.

### 3.1 Already aligned (no change)

- **Agents are behavioral specs, not "You are a senior X".** `xml-prose-format.md` mandates third-person declarative voice; `<mindset>` lines are one or two sentences of task-relevant priorities ("Measure first, optimize second"). That is the short, alignment-type steering S3 found helpful and avoids the long-persona penalty. `test_agent_structure.py::TestAgentDescriptionInterface` already bans the past-implying vocabulary that S17 tied to hallucination (F6). The `description` field states a trigger, not a biography.
- **Business panel treats experts as lenses.** `agents/business-panel-experts.md` `<mindset>`: "Each expert = analytic lens, not impersonation"; `<gotchas>` `lens-not-impersonation` forbids fabricated quotes; `MODE_Business_Panel.md` `<thinking>` "Productive Tension: Seek framework disagreement" and `<communication>` "Distinguish consensus vs majority vs outlier" implement F2's disagreement requirement.
- **Evaluation is separated and skeptical.** `agents/self-review.md` `<mindset>` "Assume flaws exist until proven otherwise" is the explicit skepticism S14 says is needed; `<finding_policy>` reports everything with severity and confidence, matching S16's code-review guidance. `commands/review.md` has a Challenge step and an evidence-based pushback protocol. This is S17's "skeptical standalone evaluator" pattern.
- **Delegation already carries the two anchoring remedies that measure.** `core/rules/RULES_DELEGATION.md` requires the delegate to return counterevidence and unknowns (S8's "comprehensive angles") and requires re-grep of every cited file:line before acting (structural, not an ignore-instruction). `user_request_verbatim` in the packet is the "explicit shared-goal commitment" S11 recommends for independent agents.
- **Delegated decisions are audited.** `commands/brainstorm.md` `<decision_modes>` tags a silent accept of the ★ option as `delegated` and forces `/sc:review --audit-delegated`. The ★ marker in `RULES_INTERACTION.md` is an anchor by design; the audit is the counterweight.
- **`/sc:prompt` already removes several framing hazards.** `removal_targets` rows for emphasis inflation, prohibition walls, severity filters, numeric caps and trait claims all follow S15/S16.
- **Negative gates are paired with positives.** Every command description's "Do NOT auto-trigger on X — those get Y" names the alternative, which is the form S15 recommends; the gate is test-enforced (`test_command_structure.py`) and was calibrated in `agent-native-design-ajitta-2026-05-31.md`. `<bounds><never>` is a fixed schema slot for hard boundaries, the one place the anecdotal negative-instruction literature agrees negatives belong.
- **Verbalized sampling refuses pre-assigned roles.** `MODE_Verbalized_Sampling.md` `<gotchas>` `pre-assign` forbids "contrarian"/"canonical" labels before generation and states probabilities are not calibrated. That is a different mechanism from the Expert Pair (which assigns roles with distinct success criteria on purpose); the two do not conflict.
- **Safety content is ordered first.** `xml-prose-format.md` "Section Ordering" places identity and `critical_*` sections first, which is the only position rule the evidence supports without numbers.

### 3.2 Gaps and conflicts

G1. **`/sc:prompt` has no persona row.** `commands/prompt.md` `<removal_targets>` catches thinking incantations and emphasis inflation but lets "You are a world-class senior engineer" through unchanged on a debugging or factual prompt. F1 is the strongest single finding of this study and the command that exists to strip folklore does not act on it. Measured on 2026-09-14: see §5.

G2. **`/sc:spec-panel` lacks the three clauses the business panel has.** `commands/spec-panel.md` names six living experts (Osmani, Hickey, Beck, Fowler, Nygard, Majors) and offers `--iterations N`, but has no `lens-not-impersonation` gotcha, no disagreement requirement, and no rule that a later iteration must surface findings the earlier one did not. The business panel carries all three. F2 says a panel without forced disagreement converges on one answer; S1's decision table names that exact failure mode for the Expert Pair.

G3. **`core/BUSINESS_SYMBOLS.md` assigns each expert a voice.** Its `<symbols>` block labels experts "Framework+Voice" (📚 Academic, 💬 Provocative, 🎲 Contrarian, 🧠 Wise), while `agents/business-panel-experts.md` says "framework lens, not literal voice" and `MODE_Business_Panel.md` `<never>` forbids "expert persona impersonation in place of framework lens". Two shipped files disagree on whether an expert is a lens or a personality; the symbols file loads on `--structured`. `content-quality.md` rule 4 (one answer location per rule) applies.

G4. **A review delegate can receive the author's rationale.** `RULES_DELEGATION.md`'s packet lists what must travel (request verbatim, scope, evidence format, stop condition) but nothing about what must not. For review and verification delegations, F3 and F4 say the author's rationale, self-assessment and prior verdict are the strongest anchors available, and S13 measured that a persuasive rationale flips verdicts even when wrong. The repo's own March research proposed exactly this isolation (CDA Axis 1). The isolation itself is available today: the context-fork study found that every Agent-tool delegate already runs in its own subprocess, and `context: fork` is only the skill-side way to get the same thing. What never shipped is a rule about what the isolated reviewer is handed, which is this proposal.

G5. **"Advisory" is an ignore-instruction.** The same packet says the sub-agent summary is "advisory". F4 says such wording measures near zero; what works is the re-grep requirement that follows it, plus fixing the acceptance criterion before the summary is read. `[R20 Success Criteria]` exists but is not tied to dispatch.

### 3.3 Observations not promoted to proposals

- `commands/estimate.md` `<examples>` shows "Relative effort L, 85% confidence". A single numeric exemplar can anchor the reported confidence (S8, S15 "unintended patterns"). No failure observed; watch item only. The "42/42 pass (baseline 40)" exemplars elsewhere are format exemplars for evidence citation and get replaced by the real count in practice.
- The always-loaded chain imports `FLAGS.md`, then `PRINCIPLES.md`, then `RULES.md`, so the safety kernel is last of the three. The only guarantee for destructive commands is `destructive_guard.py`, which is a hook, so the rule's position is belt-and-braces. Worth one eval-harness experiment (swap the order, run the canary gates `destructive-elicitation` and `conflicting-constraints`), not a change.
- S1's Domain Expert Injection (a compliance officer or a 200-employee admin reviewing a UI) has no SuperClaude surface. It is a user-side prompting habit; `requirements-analyst.md` already asks for the primary user persona at discovery time. Adding a `--lens` flag fails [R18 Necessity Test].
- `agents/simplicity-guide.md` `<influences>` names Hickey, Beck, Cunningham and Thomas. That is S17's grounded-individuality pattern, one line long; no change.
- `docs/prompt-guidelines.md` (Korean, 51 lines) already says to avoid "CRITICAL / YOU MUST / NEVER" and to prefer "Use X because Y"; consistent with S15 and S16, nothing to add.

## 4. Proposals

Ranked by evidence strength times blast radius. Each names the edit, the evidence, the [R18] answer, and the check that would prove it. All five were applied on 2026-09-14, one commit per proposal (P5 inside the P1 commit), and their checks were run afterwards; results are in §8.

### P1. Add two rows to `commands/prompt.md` `<removal_targets>` (G1)

Row A, expert persona as accuracy booster. Signal: a leading `You are a (senior|world-class|expert|principal|20-year …)` or `act as a …` clause, in either language, on a deliverable that is factual or diagnostic. Action: delete the clause and replace it with the audience and quality-bar context the command already asks for; keep a role only when the deliverable is generative or stylistic, or when the role encodes an evaluation lens with its own success criterion (an Expert Pair, a hostile reviewer), and then keep it to one sentence with no attributes the task does not use. Reason line for the row: S2 and S3 (accuracy unchanged or lower on multiple-choice knowledge benchmarks; longer personas hurt more), S4 (irrelevant attributes cost up to ~30 points). Code deliverables are thinly covered: S2 and S4 have no code task, and S3's only code evidence is the MT-Bench Coding category, ten questions scored 1–10 by an LLM judge, −0.65 for the expert persona on a figure that names no model; that is a judged-quality delta, not a correctness measurement. The repo evidence that touches code is S17: a seniority-implying agent description raised context hallucination to 5/5 on long-output Python tasks, and replacing it cut over-engineering signals 25–73%; that measured hallucination and code shape, not correctness. The row may extend to code prompts on S17 alone if it names that limit; otherwise it leaves code prompts out until measured.

Row B, embedded anchor. Signal: a prior estimate, suspected cause, or reference value stated before the ask ("probably the cache", "should take about two days", "the last run estimated 40%"). Action: ask for the model's own estimate or diagnosis first with its criterion, then present the prior as a second input to compare against, or drop it; never rewrite it as "ignore the above", which measures as ineffective (S8, S9). A prior labeled as another model's or agent's output is the strongest form (S10, level C).

[R18]: the command is not broken without these rows, but its stated mission is to strip folklore that degrades the target model, and F1 is the best-documented folklore in the set. Check: the headless probe method already recorded in the repo, in the method section of `docs/analysis/game-experience-reasoning-vault-sc-prompt-chosh1179-2026-09-10.md` and in `plain-language-output-style-chosh1179-2026-09-11.md` (`claude -p` on Opus 5, prompts from two unrelated domains, one Korean and one English, from a workspace with a project-scope install). Baseline result is in §5; the acceptance criterion is that the rewrite removes the persona clause on the factual and debugging prompts and keeps it on the editorial one, on three or more runs per prompt, with the prompt texts recorded next to the outcomes.

### P2. Bring `commands/spec-panel.md` up to the business panel's contract (G2)

Evidence: F2 (S7 agreement lever, S17), S1's decision table. [R18]: the current command can produce a consensus panel with fabricated attributions and nothing in the file prevents it; the business panel already treats that as a failure. Check: run `/sc:spec-panel --mode critique --iterations 3` on a fixture spec before and after; count distinct findings per pass and any quoted sentence attributed to a named expert.

### P3. Make `core/BUSINESS_SYMBOLS.md` consistent with the lens rule (G3)

Rename the "Expert (Framework+Voice)" block to "Expert (Framework)" and drop the voice descriptors, or rename them to lens descriptors that label the framework rather than the person. One file, no behavior claim. Check: `uv run pytest tests/unit/test_content_structure.py tests/unit/test_cross_references.py`; nothing else references the voice tags (grep confirmed).

### P4. State what a review delegate must not receive (G4, G5)

The word has three copies, in the delegation packet, in `commands/agent.md` `<gotchas>` `context-pollution`, and in `.claude/rules/gotchas/general.md` `context-leak`; the edit lands in all three, or leaves two as pointers to the packet, per `content-quality.md` rule 4 and the `find-all-copies-first` gotcha. Evidence: F3 (S13 side-by-side vs follow-up), F4 (S9 own-standard-first), S17 Harness V2, the repo's own CDA Axis 1. [R18]: without this, `/sc:review --structured` and any Workflow review fan-out are free to hand the reviewer the most persuasive anchor in the system. Check: an eval-harness canary with a planted defect plus a confident rationale, run with and without the rationale in the packet; the metric is detection rate.

Reading: probes (a) and (b) differ in language, domain and deliverable, and each ran once, so their different outcomes show that the command has no rule for the clause, not that the same prompt would flip between runs; run-to-run stability was not measured. Both runs said why the outcome was unruled: no detector row and no documented model behavior covers it. Where the clause was removed, the report told the user it could be restored at no cost, which is the opposite of what S2 and S3 measured for a factual deliverable; where the deliverable was the factual one, the clause stayed. The control behaved correctly, so P1 Row A's "keep for stylistic work" branch is already the model's default and the row would change only the factual and diagnostic cases. Probe (b) also shows the Korean-signal half of Row A is needed: the run translated the existing English detector signals and still found nothing to match. All three runs otherwise behaved as designed (placeholders for unread facts, no `--target api` config, self-check lines withheld for Opus 5). Raw outputs: session scratchpad `probes/{a,b,c}.out`, not committed.

## 7. Limitations

- No persona measurement in S2–S5 used a Claude model. S11 includes the Claude family for framing; S10 (level C) reports Claude Haiku as more anchor-sensitive than GPT-5 mini.
- The arXiv rows and S1 were re-located in raw text after review (§1). What remains inference is stated in the rows: PRISM's Figure 1 names no model and its Extraction delta disagrees with its own Table 1; S7's Table 2 values are maxima over hyperparameter settings; S9's Knowledge Enhancement result exists only in a table. S13 was read once as an extracted PDF and not re-checked.
- The §5 probe measures the rewrite layer only. Per the recorded probe method, a rewrite canary proves nothing about downstream output until the rewritten prompt is run with its `[FILL]` slots filled; that step is part of P1's acceptance check, not of this study.
- Each §5 condition ran once, and the prompt texts survive only as the descriptions in §5, so a rerun is a new measurement with reconstructed prompts rather than a replication. P1's check calls for three or more runs per prompt with the prompts recorded.

### 8.4 Downstream run of probe (b)

The rewrite layer alone proves nothing about answers, so probe (b) also ran downstream: `claude -p "<prompt>" --model claude-opus-5` from an empty directory, two runs per variant. | Variant | Items covered (run 1, run 2) | Distinct article citations |
