# Question bank

Shapes, not scripts. Adapt each to the user's own words and the last answer. Korean and English forms are given because most sessions run in one of the two.

## Six types

**Clarification**
- "When you say 'better', better for whom, measured how?" / "'더 좋다'는 누구에게, 무엇으로 재나요?"
- "Who is the very first person to use this, and what are they doing a minute before?"
- "What is explicitly *not* part of this?" / "이건 범위 밖이다, 하는 건 뭐예요?"

**Assumptions**
- "What has to be true for this to work that you haven't checked yet?"
- "This assumes you'll still want it in six months. What tells you that?"
- "Which part would hurt most if it turned out false?" / "틀렸을 때 제일 아픈 가정은 뭐예요?"

**Evidence**
- "What have you actually seen that supports this?" / "직접 본 근거가 뭐예요?"
- "How many cases is that? One vivid case is a lead, not proof."
- "What would you expect to see if this were false?"

**Viewpoints**
- "How would someone happy with the status quo describe your idea?"
- "What's the next-best use of the same time or money?" / "같은 시간을 다른 데 쓴다면?"
- "Who loses if this works?"

**Implications**
- "If this works exactly as hoped, what does it displace or break?"
- "What happens at ten times the scale? At a tenth?"
- "What's hard to undo once you start?" / "한번 하면 되돌리기 어려운 건?"

**The question itself**
- "Is 'which option' the real question, or 'whether at all'?" / "'어느 쪽'이 질문인가요, '할까 말까'가 질문인가요?"
- "If this problem disappeared tomorrow, what would you do instead?"
- "Why now?"

## Tactic templates

- Concreteness: "Walk me through the first time, step by step." / "처음 쓰는 순간을 단계별로 말해줄래요?"
- Counterexample: "Take [specific case]. How does the idea handle it?"
- Contradiction: "Earlier you said "…", and just now "…". Which one gives?" (verbatim quotes only)
- Definition: "You've used 'X' two ways. Pick one meaning for now?"
- Falsification: "What would you need to see to drop this? A number or an event?"
- Necessity: "What breaks if we leave this out?" / "이게 없으면 뭐가 깨져요?"

## Optional lenses (pick at most two, only when the idea calls for one)

- **Premortem** (risky or irreversible bets): "It's a year later and this failed. What's the most likely story?"
- **Expert lens**, asked as a question in that person's spirit:
  - Drucker: "Who is the customer, and what do they consider value?"
  - Christensen: "What job is this hired to do?"
  - Taleb: "What breaks first under stress, and do you gain or lose from volatility?"
  - Nygard: "What happens when this fails at 3 a.m.?"
  - Hickey: "What's left after removing the accidental complexity?"
  - Beck: "What's the first test that would tell you it works?"
- **SCAMPER** (product or feature ideas, in Step 3 only): substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to other use, eliminate, reverse. Ask about one letter at a time.
- **Edges** (Step 3): cheapest version · most extreme version · do nothing.

## When answers stop moving the thesis

Switch along this ring: clarification → assumptions → evidence → viewpoints → implications → the question itself. After two dry switches, say "I think we've hit the edge of what questioning can show right now" and move to Step 3 or 5.
